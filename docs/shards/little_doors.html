Little Doors

Door I — Pamela Smith × the Vibe Coding Garage

You uploaded The Body of the Artisan on 2026-02-18 and then never wrote about it again in your prompts. Smith's argument — early-modern science emerged from artisans whose handling of materials was itself epistemic — is literally the methodology of vibe coding. You handle code, you fail batches, you discover recipes, you pass technique to apprentices (Paul, the moving-company guy). One 800-word essay called "Vibe Coding as Artisanal Epistemology" would be a real contribution and you have all the source material in your filesystem already.

Door II — The Q-log convention as published method

Your MTGSLIDER Q##_*.md files have already evolved into a method: capture verbatim user input, annotate interpretation, name what was deferred. The promptarchaeology tool extends this to retrospective analysis. Nobody has published this as a method. Working title: "The Q-Log: A Notation for AI-Assisted Research." Two pages would do it. It would matter to digital humanists, prompt engineers, and your fellow vibe-coders.

Door III — Atalanta Fugiens × Will Eisner

You uploaded Will Eisner pages on 2026-02-24 with the prompt "alchemical emblems as sequential art." This is a real research thesis waiting for someone to write it. Maier's emblems were proto-comics: image + epigram + discourse + fugue, four redundant encodings. Eisner formalized sequential art three centuries later. The connection is rigorous and underexplored. This is the kind of paper that gets you invited to a conference panel.

How to use this list

Pick one door. Spend 30 to 90 minutes on it. The point of these doors is that they are already framed by your own past prompts — you're not starting from cold; you're picking up a thought you already had.

If a door tires you, close it without guilt. The corpus will offer it again.

If a door pulls you for two hours, that's diagnostic — that one was alive. Note which one.

The Glimmung opens many doors. The pot-healer chooses one.
