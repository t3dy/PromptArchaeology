20 Questions on NSFRIPPER

Your NES audio extraction project. Two thousand hot prompts in 2024-06 and 2024-07 alone. 8,900 MIDI files. Coach offerings, not assignments.

  1. Your peak NES months were 2024-06 (1,380 prompts) and 2024-07 (1,380). What was happening in your life then that allowed two consecutive months of obsession? Notice the conditions — you might want them again.

  2. "Two DSP codebases drift apart with every fix" — you've said this multiple times. Without solving it: which of the two codebases would you keep if forced to drop one tonight?

  3. Castlevania III comes up most often in your NES prompts. Is it actually your favorite extraction, or just the one you cite reflexively?

  4. You have 321 game extractions. Name the three whose music you can hum from memory right now. Those are your real NSFRIPPER catalog.

  5. 2026-02-25: "help me find videos on YouTube to understand chiptune hacking for nes games." You were still actively learning two months ago. NSFRIPPER is not "done" in your head; the question is whether it should be.

  8. "Length-counter noise gating", "triangle gate-off DAC hold", "$4015 NSF init" — your NSFRIPPER vocabulary is publishable. Have you considered just dumping these architecture rules into a single page and posting it? Other NES audio researchers would care.

  9. The JSFX synth vs. stems pipeline divergence: is the divergence audible to you, or only to your code's diff tool? If only to the diff: maybe the diff is wrong, not the audio.

  15. tools/disk_budget/ is in your scaffolding. Has it ever actually fired in time to prevent a crisis, or has it always been retrospective?

  16. There are NES communities — NESDev, Romhacking.net, the Chipmusic.org forum. Do any of them know you've been doing this work? (Not as a should-share — as a fact-check.)
