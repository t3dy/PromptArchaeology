TIREDPROMPTS — A copy-paste library for token-burn when fried

Date: 2026-04-25 Use: When you sit down post-school and need a prompt fast. Pick by energy level + intent. Paste, react, walk away.

LOW energy — read & react (you generate ~10 words)

  1. Read [file path]. Tell me in 3 bullet points what it does. End with one question I should ask myself about it.
  2. git log --since="1 week ago" --oneline. Summarize the past week in 100 words. What's the throughline?
  3. I'm tired. In 200 words, walk me through what we did in our last session and where we left off.
  4. Take my last voice note: [paste]. Compress to 5 bullet points. Don't add anything.
  5. Look at C:\Dev\<project>. Tell me what's stale and what's hot. No advice — just observations.
  6. Read [file]. Roast it gently. Three things that are weak.
  7. Quiz me on [topic I claimed to learn last week]. Five questions. I'll guess; you grade.
  8. Diff my LEARNING_JOURNAL.md from a month ago vs today. What did I learn?
  9. Pick the most over-engineered file in [project] and explain in plain English why.
  10. What's the smallest possible thing I could do in [project] in 20 minutes?
  11. Read this paragraph I wrote: [paste]. Translate it into something a smart 10-year-old would understand.
  12. Show me the first 30 lines of [unfamiliar file]. Annotate every block.
  13. Look at my MEMORY_INDEX.md. Which entries feel stale? Just list them.
  14. Open [project]/PARKING_LOT.md. Which 3 entries should I delete because they're dead?
  15. Read the 5 most recent files in [dir]. What was I obsessed with?

MEDIUM energy — analyze & decide (you generate ~50 words)

  1. Read [project]/CLAUDE.md. Identify the 3 instructions that are too vague to act on. Suggest tighter wording.
  2. Compare these two files [A] [B]. What's redundant? What's contradictory?
  3. Look at my git history for [project]. Identify the moment I switched directions and explain why I might have.
  4. Read [doc]. Now read [doc 2]. Are they consistent? Where do they diverge?
  5. Take this rough idea: [paste]. Draft three different framings — minimal, ambitious, esoteric. I'll pick.
  6. Survey the tools/ directory in [project]. Which scripts have I never run? Which look broken?
  7. Audit the SQLite schema in [project]/db.sqlite. What field is misnamed? What table should be split?
  8. Look at this Python file: [path]. Suggest one refactor under 20 lines that would meaningfully improve it.
  9. Read [old planning doc]. Write a 3-sentence "what we should have done" retrospective.
  10. My bash history shows I ran [X] command 12 times this week. Why might I be doing that? Suggest a script.

HIGH energy — design & commit (save these for fresh days)

  1. I want to add [feature] to [project]. Run /plan-joe-chip-scope. Frozen scope only — no extras.
  2. Run /plan-deckard-boundary on [decision]. I need to know what's in vs. out of LLM territory.
  3. Design a vertical slice for [feature]. Use /plan-runciter-slice format. Include acceptance gate.
  4. I'm choosing between [A] and [B] architecturally. Run /plan-bohlen-constraint to surface what constrains the decision.
  5. Stand up the smallest end-to-end version of [project] in one file. Constraint: under 150 lines.

Crisis prompts — when the urge to start something new hits

  1. I want to start a new project called [X]. Talk me out of it. Be specific about what existing project this rhymes with.
  2. I'm about to make a new skill called /plan-[name]. Steel-man and then attack. Should I park it instead?
  3. I want to add a THE_*.md to [project]. Diagnose the urge. What am I avoiding?
  4. I keep wanting to refactor [X]. Is the urge real or aesthetic? Five questions to clarify.
  5. I'm tempted to rebuild [project] from scratch. Read the existing version first. Veto if appropriate.

Joy prompts — for the laughs

  1. Show me three weird Magic cards from MTG history I probably don't know. Tell me what's funny about each.
  2. Pick a PKD novel I haven't mentioned. Pitch me a one-paragraph adaptation set in [random domain].
  3. Generate a fake hermetic axiom that sounds Renaissance but is actually about software bugs.
  4. Translate one of my existing Python functions into a parable. Keep it short.
  5. What would [my project] look like if it were a Bach fugue? Map components to voices.
  6. Roast my project naming choices in the style of a 17th-century alchemist who is unimpressed.
  7. Write a 4-line poem in the voice of one of my dogs about my coding habits.
  8. Generate one absurd MTG card whose mechanic depends on knowing my CLAUDE.md instructions.
  9. Imagine a future archeologist excavating C:\Dev. Write their field notes.
  10. Give me a tarot card whose meaning is "this PR has too many files."

Archaeology prompts — visiting old work

  1. What's in [old project] that I've forgotten? Briefing in 300 words.
  2. Pick the most interesting file in [old project] I haven't touched in 3 months. Tell me why it's interesting.
  3. Read [old conversation log]. What was I trying to figure out? Did I figure it out?
  4. Diff [old project]'s structure 6 months ago vs now. What got abandoned?
  5. What promise did I make myself in [old README] that I haven't kept? No judgment, just observation.

Cleaning prompts — useful junk-drawer work

  1. Find all TODO and FIXME comments across [project]. Bucket them: real, dead, aspirational. I'll triage your buckets.
  2. Find duplicate-ish files across [project]. Suggest merges.
  3. Look at my dependencies. Which ones could I remove?
  4. Find files I've imported but never actually use.
  5. Generate a .gitignore review — what should be in it that isn't?

Tutoring prompts — convert tokens to knowledge

  1. Explain [CS concept] using a metaphor from my interests (PKD / alchemy / MTG / NES audio).
  2. Show me three different ways to write [function I just wrote]. Explain tradeoffs.
  3. Walk me through how SQLite stores a B-tree. Use diagrams in ASCII.
  4. Read [Python file in my project]. Quiz me on what it does. Five questions.
  5. Pick a design pattern I'm probably using accidentally. Name it and show me how to use it deliberately.

Usage rules

  • Don't pick more than ONE prompt per session.
  • If a prompt's output makes you want to start something — /plan-abendsen-parking it.
  • Save outputs you like into LEARNING_JOURNAL.md or IDEAS.md.
  • If you're tempted to "just one more prompt" past 90 minutes, /clear instead.
  • These prompts are tools, not assignments. Most of them you'll never use. The point is to have them ready when the brain fog says "what should I even do."
