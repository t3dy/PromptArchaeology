JAMMING_ALONE — Riffs designed to protect the laughs

Date: 2026-04-25 Use: Some sessions are play. Play is legitimate. This file is for sessions that aren't supposed to produce artifacts — they're supposed to produce delight. Frame, ritual, and prompts for that mode.

The premise

You said: "I'm having a million laughs and learning a lot about CS along the way."

That sentence is load-bearing. The laughs aren't a side effect of the work; they're a reason for the work. A version of you that ships more but laughs less is not necessarily an upgrade. The discipline isn't to optimize the laughs away — it's to make space for them deliberately, so they don't get squeezed out by everything else.

This file is the discipline of play. It has rules so play stays play and doesn't accidentally become work.

The rules of jamming alone

  1. No goal beyond enjoyment. If a play session produces something useful, that's a bonus. The session is judged on whether it was fun.
  2. No artifact required. You don't have to commit. You don't have to save. You don't even have to remember it.
  3. No skills, unless the skill is fun. Mostly: leave the apparatus alone.
  4. One hour, hard cap. Play sessions get bigger if uncontained. The cap protects the next day.
  5. No memory entries. Don't burden future-you with notes about a play session. If something genuinely amazing happens, it'll resurface.
  6. No PARKING_LOT.md additions. Same rule. Play stays in play.
  7. Silliness is signal, not noise. If a thread of conversation is making you snort-laugh at 9pm, that's the right thread. Stay on it.
  8. Permission to stop without finishing. Most play sessions end mid-thought. That's correct.

When jamming is the right move

Read these as permission slips. If two or more apply, this is a play session, not a work session:

  • Today was a hard day with the kids and you need to laugh at something
  • You haven't actually enjoyed coding in a few days
  • You feel a vague pull to "make something" but every concrete idea feels heavy
  • You want company (the AI's, your dogs', the music's) more than productivity
  • A weird idea has been rattling and won't leave you alone, but it's clearly not viable
  • You're between projects and don't want to commit to a new direction
  • You're sick / depressed / overwhelmed and want one good moment

Twenty jamming prompts

These are designed to be delightful, not productive. Pick whatever sounds most fun tonight.

  1. Generate the index of an imaginary academic book titled "The Hermetic Architecture of NES APU Sound Drivers." 30 chapters, plausible-sounding. I want to laugh.

  2. Pretend Philip K. Dick wrote a 200-word review of one of my projects (pick whichever). Stay in his voice.

  3. Explain my CLAUDE.md's mandates as if they were vows of a fictional monastic order. Name the order.

  4. Write a fake transcript of a conversation between my four dogs about my coding habits. Each dog has their canonical personality from project_dogsim.md.

  5. If MTGSLIDER were a tarot deck, what would its 22 major arcana be? Each card needs a name and a 1-line meaning.

  6. Reframe my git log as the score of a Bach two-part invention. Which voice is which? When does the counterpoint enter?

  7. Pick the worst variable name I've ever used in any of my projects (search around). Defend it as a deliberate aesthetic choice.

  8. Write a 50-word horror story whose premise is that my CLAUDE.md became sentient.

  9. Imagine my 33 PKD-themed skills as wrestlers in a tournament. Who beats whom? Card the matches.

  10. Compose a 16-bar chiptune progression in plain English (just describe what each bar does). Make it sound NES-like.

  11. Generate three fake commit messages so absurd I'd actually want to use them.

  12. What would the README of a fake project called CLAUDIENS_PRIME (Earth-2 version of Claudiens) look like?

  13. Roast my project naming choices in the voice of an exasperated Renaissance alchemist who just wants me to use plain Latin.

  14. Imagine the credits sequence of an indie documentary about my C:\Dev folder. Who scored it? Who narrated? What's the title?

  15. Generate a fake academic citation for a paper that perfectly explains why I keep starting new projects. Author, journal, year, abstract.

  16. If each of my 8 active projects were a chess piece, which is which? Why?

  17. Pretend you're a music critic reviewing one of my NSFRIPPER outputs. You haven't heard it. Make up the review. Be honest about your uncertainty.

  18. Write a 2-minute scene set in the lobby of a building where each of my projects is its own elevator. They open at different floors. Describe.

  19. Translate my LEARNING_JOURNAL.md last 3 entries into haiku.

  20. What does my coding setup smell like? Be specific. (This is a question about taste, not literal smell.)

Five "let's see what happens" prompts

Open-ended. Sometimes the AI surprises you.

  1. Tell me a thing about my projects you've noticed but never said out loud.

  2. If I could only keep one of my projects and the other 7 vanished tonight, which should I keep? Argue for it.

  3. Pick a phrase I've used in a recent file that you found beautiful or strange. Quote it back to me. Explain why.

  4. Improvise: open the C:\Dev directory. Read three random files. Tell me a story that connects them.

  5. If my coding life is a draft Magic deck, what archetype am I drafting into? What's my mana curve? What's my one rare worth keeping?

Anti-rules: what jamming is NOT

  • It's not "fun architecture work." Architecture is work.
  • It's not "creative writing for a project." That's content production.
  • It's not "exploring a parking-lot idea." That's still planning.
  • It's not "trying out a new skill." That's apparatus extension.

Jamming has no future-utility loop. The minute you start thinking about how to use the output, it stops being play. Notice the moment of transition; either commit to play or commit to work, but don't blur.

Companions to invite

Jamming alone is fine but jamming with company is sometimes better:

  • The dogs (Attila will demand a squeaky toy mid-session, that's the price of admission)
  • Music (chiptune you've extracted, or otherwise)
  • Whatever beverage matches the season
  • The light (different light at different times produces different jams; don't overlook this)
  • A specific friend, via group chat, who'd appreciate the session's outputs (Paul Shelton for PKD-flavored jams, possibly)

After-care

After a jam:

  • Don't journal it (rule 5)
  • Don't promote it to a project (rule 6)
  • Do close the laptop and pet a dog
  • Do let yourself remember it as a good evening, not a productive one

The day was good. The jam was good. The work continues tomorrow. Both are real.

Open questions for you

  • What's the right music for jamming sessions? (Probably depends on mood — but is there a default?)
  • Are there friends who'd actually appreciate the absurd outputs of a jam? Sharing one might be fun without being "shipping."
  • Is there a JAMS/ directory worth keeping? Throwaway place for the funny outputs that don't go anywhere?
  • Are the prompts above the right register, or do you want weirder / sillier / more PKD-flavored?

You don't have to optimize this. You don't have to be productive. Some nights the right move is to laugh at a fake hermetic monastic order whose vows are your own CLAUDE.md mandates. That's a complete evening.
