Questions the coach can ask you

Random pulls from this list. No question requires answering — the point is to feel them, not to deliver. Some are easy and some are sharp; both are useful.

On scholarly values

  1. What's a topic you've spent 100+ prompts on that no one in your life knows about?
  2. What scholarly tradition do you most identify with right now?
  3. What's an academic question your prompts keep circling but never commit to?
  4. What's the sentence about your work you'd most want a future reader to find?
  5. Whose scholarship do you most argue with in your own head?
  6. What primary text have you returned to most often across years of prompts?
  7. Which of your databases would you save first if you could only save one?
  8. What's a citation you would make confidently in print but only mention obliquely in prompts?
  9. What's the most interesting thing you know that no one's published?
  10. What's a value you act on consistently but have never written down?

On builderly values

  1. What's the smallest version of your favorite project that would still be interesting?
  2. What's a tool you built that turned out to be more useful than you expected?
  3. What's a tool you built that you never actually use?
  4. What's a pattern you keep reinventing because you forget you already solved it?
  5. Which of your projects feels the most like yours — like no one else would have made it?
  6. What's a constraint you set yourself that turned out to be the best decision in a project?
  7. What's a feature you're proud of that no end-user would ever notice?
  8. What's a refactor you've fantasized about for months without doing?
  9. What language or library would you learn next if energy were free?
  10. What's the most interesting failure mode in any of your projects?

On the corpus itself

  1. What did you say in an old conversation that you'd push back on now?
  2. When did you first use the word that's now central to your work? Want to find out?
  3. What's a topic in your prompts that has fewer mentions than you'd guess?
  4. What's a topic in your prompts that has more mentions than you'd guess?
  5. Which year of your prompts was the most exploratory? The most operational?
  6. What's a question you asked the AI three different ways before finding the right framing?
  7. Which of your conversations would make the best book chapter as-is?
  8. Which prompt cascade are you most curious to revisit?
  9. What was the longest stretch you went without writing about [chosen topic]?
  10. What's the most-clarified idea in your prompt history?

On rhythm and energy

  1. What's a pattern in your tired-mode prompts you'd like to interrupt?
  2. What's a kind of session you used to have a lot that you don't have anymore?
  3. What's a session shape that consistently leaves you feeling good?
  4. When in the week do you produce your weirdest ideas?
  5. What's a project that got more interesting after you took a break from it?

On companionship

  1. What's a thought you've never told anyone but have told the AI?
  2. Which of your projects feels lonely?
  3. Which of your projects is secretly for one specific person?
  4. Who would understand your work most easily? Have you told them?
  5. What would change if you sent one prompt-cascade fragment to one friend tomorrow?

On the future-you

  1. What does Future-You wish Past-You had written down?
  2. What would you tell a stranger to read first if they wanted to know you?
  3. What's a project you'd be glad existed even if no one ever used it?
  4. What's a project that would still be valuable if you stopped working on it tomorrow?
  5. What's a thing you keep planning to learn that you might never need?

Sharp questions (use sparingly)

  1. What's a project you keep working on that you no longer believe in?
  2. What's a project you've been avoiding that you secretly know is the real one?
  3. What's a question you've been asking the AI when you should be asking a person?
  4. What's the version of your work that exists only because of fatigue?
  5. What's the question you're afraid your prompt history is asking?
