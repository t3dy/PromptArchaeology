The Deck of Discovery

Random cards in the spirit of Brian Eno's Oblique Strategies, tuned to prompt archaeology. The coach pulls one. You react or don't.

  • Read your most recent prompt as if a stranger wrote it.
  • Find a prompt where you said "wait" and consider what you almost did.
  • The shortest cascade is also valid.
  • Pick the project you've avoided this week. Open one of its files. Just look.
  • Walk away. Make a sandwich. Return only if a thought returns first.
  • Underline a single phrase. That's the session.
  • What you ignored in 2023 is now what you're working on.
  • Look for a question you answered without realizing.
  • Three projects, one hand. Drop two.
  • Trust the corpus. Distrust the plan.
  • Search for "actually" and read three results aloud.
  • The first prompt of any conversation already contains the answer.
  • Your typos are diagnostic.
  • Find a sentence that sounds like someone you admire and decide if you want to keep sounding like them.
  • Pick a date at random. Show me what you were thinking that day.
  • The cascade that bored you is the one to revisit.
  • Your shortest prompt has the most pressure inside it.
  • Make a small mistake on purpose.
  • One word you used too much. One word you didn't use enough.
  • Pretend a different version of you wrote your last prompt. What were they trying to do?
  • The corpus knows things you don't.
  • Honor the unfinished thought.
  • Skip ahead. Then skip back. Notice what you noticed.
  • The prompt is the artifact. The reply is the wind.
  • What if the right next move is to read instead of write?
  • What you wrote at midnight has texture that morning-you doesn't have. Don't sand it.
  • Open a 1000-page cascade at chunk 7. Don't start at chunk 0.
  • Compare two prompts on the same topic from different years. Side by side.
  • Find a prompt you'd put in a museum.
  • Find a prompt you'd hide.
  • The Glimmung is not a productivity manager.
  • Joe Fernwright restored one pot at a time.
  • Heldscalla rises slowly or not at all. Either is fine.
