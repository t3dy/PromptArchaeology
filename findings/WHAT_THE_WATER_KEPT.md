# What the Water Kept

*Recurring obsessions surfaced from sixteen years of prompts. Each is documented by date and frequency. Ordered by surprise, not by volume.*

---

## 1. The recurring saint: Atalanta Fugiens

Across the corpus there are **two thousand-page conversations** on Michael Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* (1617). One alone is 988 pages. They span 2024-09-16 onward. You were translating epigrams, asking about Hermes Trismegistus references, planning emblem analysis "as sequential art" via Will Eisner, comparing your work to the Furnace and Fugue project by Jennifer Nummedal.

Frequency check: "atalanta" + "maier" appears in prompts going back to **2010**. This is a 16-year throughline.

**Why it kept:** Maier solved a multimodal problem — emblem + epigram + discourse + fugue — that you keep trying to solve in your own register (the digital humanities site, the relational browser, the tabular knowledge portal). The Atalanta cascade isn't a project; it's an apprenticeship.

## 2. PKD's Exegesis as a field site

PKD spikes appear in **2010-11, 2011-12, 2024-09, 2025-08**. The 2025-08 spike alone has **4,824 PKD prompts** in a single month. You returned to specific Exegesis fragments multiple times — Plotinus, Burroughs, the "language elves" passage, the Maze of Death Theology, the orthogonal-time gloss.

**Why it kept:** PKD is the only writer in your corpus you cite *like a primary source* — chapter and verse. (You said it explicitly in 2024-11: *"as in [4:103]"*.) Your QueryPat / PKD Knowledge Portal is the obvious next step; the corpus has been building its prerequisites for fifteen years.

## 3. Aristotle's habits → the intellect's habits

**2025-05-07** (Aristotle on Habits and the Intellect): *"one thing I've noticed again and again teaching the metaphysical aspects of Aristotle's ethics is that students don't have a sense of how habits accrue and add to your being. And the thing is, they really don't know that giving the intellect habits does this either."*

This is also a prompt about **CS** — though you didn't say so. The disposition you wish students could feel about ethical habit-formation is the same disposition you keep trying to form in yourself about coding habit-formation. That you teach Aristotle to elementary kids while building your own intellect's habits with an AI is one of the load-bearing arrangements of your week.

## 4. Earned high-low synthesis

A pattern across years:

- 2014: *"Esoteric Book Chat is actually Wayne's World"*
- 2015: *"Aleister Crowley Hello Kitty memes"*
- 2016: *"goofy decks like my Rubinia Soulsinger EDH deck in progress"*
- 2024: *"Build me a Claude skill that can build custom Python scripts that can access the Scryfall API to search card info and fetch card art, then put the results into a slideshow and pdf file (same information two different formats). I am interested in doing videos on historical magic"*
- 2026: *"Magic creator content tool — read 17lands data with win rates, download Scryfall art, make a slideshow"*

**Why it kept:** This is a coherent product instinct, not a quirk. *Take a serious source. Add the gloss of casual play. Make it a slideshow.* It works in MTGSLIDER. It works in Claudiens. It would work in a podcast. It's also why you watch "Limited Level-Ups" and want to make tools for that audience.

## 5. The educational-materials business

## 6. Marxism as background radiation

Marxism never spikes high (max ~50 prompts/month) but it **never goes away**. From 2009 to 2026 there is no calendar quarter without Marxist prompts. The mode is steady, low, persistent — the way a daily reader behaves with their actual canon.

When you talk about your scholarly traditions, Marxism may not be the first you mention. The corpus disagrees with you. It is one of *the* most consistent presences.

## 7. NES audio as a 2024 obsession

NES/chiptune appears occasionally for fifteen years and then **explodes 2024-06 (1,380) and 2024-07 (1,380)**. Two months. You were inside NSFRIPPER. Then it falls back to ~50/month.

**Why it kept:** Unlike alchemy or PKD which have wide-temporal hum, NES is a *project interval* — when the work is hot, it's everything; when it's cold, it's a back-burner. This is a different shape from your other obsessions. It's the only one that behaves like a campaign.

## 8. Dogs as a real-life baseline

The "dogs" column in the corpus tracks not just your dogs but the SMS data — texts about dogs with friends, family, partner. Big spikes: **2020-11 (772), 2021-12 (1,450), 2022-07 (2,123), 2025-04 (708), 2025-12 (1,391)**.

This isn't a project. This is a life. The corpus contains it because you treat your prompt history as a complete record of being-Ted, not as a research notebook. That choice means you have something most digital humanists don't: ground truth for *how project obsessions interleave with the life that holds them*.

## 9. Susan Sontag's "every kind of life"

You bookmarked the same Sontag quote **twice** (2010-12-18, 2015-08-15): *"What I really wanted was every kind of life, and the writer's life seemed the most inclusive."*

This is the **only quote you've bookmarked twice** in the corpus that passes the "looks like a value statement" filter. It's the closest thing to a self-chosen epigraph you have.

## 10. The recovery-from-context-loss pattern

Multiple times across the corpus: *"We had a catastrophic failure in another window. Here's what's been happening please [paste]."*

You wrote this in 2024, 2025, 2026. It's a methodology you've evolved: when one session collapses, you **paste the corpus of the dead session into a new one as boot context**. This is a real epistemic move — treating LLM sessions as crash-recoverable processes — and you invented it under pressure. It's worth naming and codifying. (The Q-log convention from MTGSLIDER is its evolved form.)

## What the water kept that you keep too

If you were summarizing your scholarly values from this list alone, the values are:

1. **Bring high-cultural objects into reach.** Not popularize; not dilute; *make queryable*. Lower heaven.
2. **Process > product, because process compounds.** The table is an exercise in tabling. The slideshow is an exercise in slideshow-ing. The product is the residue.
3. **Earnestness through irony.** Wayne's World is acceptable footwear for a serious cathedral.
4. **Treat your own work as a primary source.** PKD did. So did Maier. So do you, when you re-read your prompts.
5. **Refuse the boundary between work and life.** Dog-walking on voice mode. Aristotle for the elementary classroom. Pet behavior in the canon files.
6. **Build for one specific audience.** MTG creators. PKD readers. Paul Shelton. The moving-company guy.
7. **Methodology emerges under pressure.** Catastrophic-failure recovery becomes a skill becomes a Q-log becomes promptarchaeology.

The water keeps what's heavy. These are heavy.
